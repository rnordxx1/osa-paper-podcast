Every day, one new paper on obstructive sleep apnea surgery: hypoglossal nerve stimulation, palate surgery including barbed reposition pharyngoplasty, tongue base, skeletal and nasal procedures, patient selection and comparators. Every episode summarizes the paper, walks through it as a teaching round, places it against a 440-paper evidence library, and ends with whether it should change practice. Episodes are written and narrated by an AI system working from the full text of each paper and the host's curated library of digests, under rules set by the host. They are reviewed for teaching, not for patient care. Nothing here is medical advice. Episode guide and subscribe links: https://www.sleepapneaimplant.org/podcast Produced by Ryan Nord, MD (VCU Health). Episodes

  • 2026-10-14 — Post-Operative Radiological Evaluation of a Bilateral Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation System · 0:14:00
    A single-center German case series of eleven patients asked what the first postoperative image should be after implanting the Genio bilateral hypoglossal nerve stimulator. Plain x-rays showed the hardware cleanly in every patient, while every computed tomography scan carried metal artifact, and the paper reported no clinical outcomes linking paddle position to function. The take-home point: plain films make a sensible day-one baseline, and repeat imaging over time is needed to show whether the paddles migrate.

    Kilgue A, Gehl H-B, Abolmaali N, Scholtz L-U, Scherl C, Todt I. "Post-operative radiological evaluation of a bilateral hypoglossal nerve stimulation system." *Front. Surg.* 2026;13:1818247.   • 2026-10-14 — Exploring the Role of Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy in Optimizing Upper Airway Stimulation Therapy for Non-Responders · 0:13:45
    Copper's group at Sint Antonius Hospital in the Netherlands described how they use drug-induced sleep endoscopy with the stimulator switched on in patients who still fail Inspire hypoglossal nerve stimulation after standard titration, and what happened to those patients afterwards. In a small retrospective series without a control group, patients who had settings changes or added therapy and returned for follow-up saw their apnea-hypopnea index fall by about 18 events per hour. Dr. Nord considers the procedure a good tool for the tolerant non-responder once awake endoscopy and reprogramming have failed, while noting that the series cannot show the endoscopy itself caused the improvement.

    Kant E, Hardeman JA, Stokroos RJ, Copper MP. Exploring the role of drug-induced sleep endoscopy in optimizing upper airway stimulation therapy for non-responders. *Sleep Breath*. 2025;29(5). doi:10.1007/s11325-025-03468-z
  • 2026-10-14 — GLP-1 receptor agonists for obstructive sleep apnea: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials · 0:14:28
    A meta-analysis in Sleep and Breathing pooled randomized trials of liraglutide and tirzepatide in adults with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea. The drugs lowered the apnea-hypopnea index by about ten events per hour more than control, but the effect depended heavily on the drug, and pooled oxygen measures did not improve significantly. The paper does not change who is offered surgery, but it sharpens counseling: quote drug-specific numbers, re-test after weight loss, and, as in Dr. Nord's practice, treat weight-loss drugs and Inspire as complementary parts of treatment, though no trial has yet tested them together.

    Carvalhal G, Mora MMR, Peralta-Jiménez GA, Ayasa L, Barrera V, Advani K, et al. GLP-1 receptor agonists for obstructive sleep apnea: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. *Sleep Breath*. 2026. doi:10.1007/s11325-026-03826-5
  • 2026-10-14 — Edema and Subjective Discomfort After Barbed Stayed Bridge Pharyngoplasty (BSBP) · 0:13:31
    Researchers at Sapienza University of Rome followed a small, carefully selected group of patients with moderate obstructive sleep apnea after barbed stayed bridge pharyngoplasty, a muscle-sparing barbed palate operation, to measure early pain, swelling and discomfort. Pain stayed low, averaging about three out of ten on the first day and near zero by one month, with no bleeding, but without a comparison group the study cannot show the technique outperforms barbed reposition pharyngoplasty. Dr. Nord's take-home point: a promising early-recovery signal for one technique in thirty selected patients, still awaiting a head-to-head comparison with barbed reposition pharyngoplasty.

    Pace A, Iannella G, Maniaci A, Cocuzza S, Moffa A, Alunni Fegatelli D, et al. Edema and Subjective Discomfort After Barbed Stayed Bridge Pharyngoplasty (BSBP). *JCM*. 2025;14(20):7402. doi:10.3390/jcm14207402
  • 2026-10-14 — Hypoxic Burden Reduction After Lateral Pharyngoplasty in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea - Beyond the Apnea-Hypopnea Index · 0:13:54
    A single-center study from the Pontifical Catholic University of Campinas in Brazil asked whether lateral pharyngoplasty lowers hypoxic burden, a measure of nighttime oxygen loss, and not just the apnea-hypopnea index. In the 27 patients with hypoxic burden measured before and after surgery, median hypoxic burden fell to about a third of baseline. It also fell significantly in patients who failed by apnea-hypopnea index criteria, but the study had no control group and measured no cardiovascular outcomes. Verdict: watch — it supports reporting hypoxic burden alongside the apnea-hypopnea index after palate surgery, without changing who is offered surgery.

    Vieira ALP, Duarte BB, Almeida AR, Greters ME, Leandro-Merhi VA, de Aquino JLB. "Hypoxic burden reduction after lateral pharyngoplasty in patients with obstructive sleep apnea: Beyond the apnea-hypopnea index." *Sleep and Breathing* (2026) 30:267. DOI: 10.1007/s11325-026-03808-7. PMID: not printed in PDF (42771120 per the weekly PubMed scan). Received 25 May 2026; revised 6 September 2026; accepted 14 September 2026. Open access (CC BY 4.0).
  • 2026-10-11 — Ultrasonographic Tongue Base Motion Does Not Correlate With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Outcomes · 0:14:37
    A two-center Swiss study asked whether the way the tongue base moves on awake ultrasound during hypoglossal nerve stimulation predicts how much a patient's obstructive sleep apnea improves. In 64 patients with unilateral Inspire implants, no ultrasound measure of tongue movement size, symmetry or shape was significantly associated with the drop in the apnea-hypopnea index, though the confidence intervals were wide. The call is no change in practice, with ultrasound worth watching as a research tool.

    Tschopp S, Janjic V, Caversaccio M, Borner U, Tschopp K. Ultrasonographic Tongue Base Motion Does Not Correlate With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Outcomes. *Laryngoscope Investig Oto*. 2026;11(2). doi:10.1002/lio2.70376
  • 2026-10-10 — Auditory Perceptions Associated With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:46
    A single-center telephone survey of 70 Inspire hypoglossal nerve stimulation recipients asked whether patients hear sounds when the device stimulates. Twelve of 70 (17.1 percent) reported a new sound during stimulation, most often described as buzzing, followed by humming and electrical, mostly mild, with none stopping therapy; the study was too small to show whether preexisting tinnitus, age, body mass index or device settings matter. The call is a narrow change: ask about auditory symptoms and include them in counseling, with no change to candidacy or surgical technique.

    Belilos A, Adibi I, Tucker A, Saeedi A, Calder A, Lee L, Nord R. "Auditory Perceptions Associated With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea." *Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology* 2026;11:e70550 (6 pages). DOI: 10.1002/lio2.70550 (as printed). PMID: not printed in PDF (42694569 per the weekly PubMed scan). Received 8 January 2026; revised 28 May 2026; accepted 21 August 2026. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND).   • 2026-10-07 — Oropharyngeal Lateral Wall Collapse on Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy as a Predictor of Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Response in Obstructive Sleep Apnoea-A Systematic Review · 0:22:43
    A systematic review of eight Inspire cohorts, 1,051 patients in all, asked whether complete collapse of the lateral pharyngeal walls on sleep endoscopy predicts a poor response to hypoglossal nerve stimulation. It lowers the response rate by roughly 15 to 20 percentage points, yet about two of three such patients still respond, so it is a counseling point rather than an exclusion. Call: watch.

    McBrinn S, Skan O, Iacovidou A. "Oropharyngeal Lateral Wall Collapse on Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy as a Predictor of Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Response in Obstructive Sleep Apnoea—A Systematic Review." *Diagnostics* 2026;16:2529 (20 pages; issue number not printed).   • 2026-10-05 — Consensus Statements Among European Sleep Surgery Experts on Tongue, Hypopharynx, and Supraglottis Associated With Snoring and Obstructive Sleep Apnea - Part 1 - Evaluation and Decision Making · 0:16:51
    A European expert panel reached consensus on how to evaluate the tongue base, hypopharynx and supraglottis in snoring and sleep apnea, and how those findings should steer treatment. The statements support staging palate surgery before hypoglossal nerve stimulation when large tonsils coexist with tongue base collapse, and repeating sleep endoscopy before any revision surgery. Call: watch; the workup stays as it is.

    Olszewska E, De Vito A, Baptista P, Delakorda M, Heiser C, Cheong RCT, et al. Consensus Statements Among European Sleep Surgery Experts on Tongue, Hypopharynx, and Supraglottis Associated with Snoring and Obstructive Sleep Apnea: Part 1: Evaluation and Decision Making. *JCM*. 2025;15(1):80. doi:10.3390/jcm15010080
