Every day, one new paper on obstructive sleep apnea surgery: hypoglossal nerve stimulation, palate surgery including barbed reposition pharyngoplasty, tongue base, skeletal and nasal procedures, patient selection and comparators. Every episode summarizes the paper, walks through it as a teaching round, places it against a 440-paper evidence library, and ends with whether it should change practice. Episodes are written and narrated by an AI system working from the full text of each paper and the host's curated library of digests, under rules set by the host. They are reviewed for teaching, not for patient care. Nothing here is medical advice. Episodes

  • 2026-10-09 — Ultrasonographic Tongue Base Motion Does Not Correlate With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Outcomes · 0:10:13
    A Swiss study of 64 patients with unilateral Inspire II hypoglossal nerve stimulators asked whether tongue base motion seen on awake submental ultrasound predicts improvement in the apnea-hypopnea index. None of the ultrasound features, including forward movement, bilateral movement and tongue shape, showed a detectable association with apnea-hypopnea index reduction, though confidence intervals were wide. The call is no change: visible tongue motion is not a demonstrated predictor of outcome, and there is no support for using it as a titration target, while prospective ultrasound paired with airway endoscopy is worth watching.

    Tschopp S, Janjic V, Caversaccio M, Borner U, Tschopp K. Ultrasonographic Tongue Base Motion Does Not Correlate With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Outcomes. *Laryngoscope Investig Oto*. 2026;11(2). doi:10.1002/lio2.70376
  • 2026-10-08 — Exploring the Role of Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy in Optimizing Upper Airway Stimulation Therapy for Non-Responders · 0:13:56
    Kant and colleagues reviewed 29 non-responders to Inspire upper airway stimulation who underwent drug-induced sleep endoscopy with the device switched on, to guide changes in stimulation settings or additional treatment. Among the 18 patients with follow-up, 8 became responders, mostly after device-setting changes or barbed reposition pharyngoplasty, but there was no control group and, as the authors note, more than half were not effectively treated. The call is watch: it is a reasonable option for tolerant non-responders, but it is not yet proven to add benefit.

    Kant E, Hardeman JA, Stokroos RJ, Copper MP. Exploring the role of drug-induced sleep endoscopy in optimizing upper airway stimulation therapy for non-responders. *Sleep Breath*. 2025;29(5). doi:10.1007/s11325-025-03468-z
  • 2026-10-07 — Oropharyngeal Lateral Wall Collapse on Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy as a Predictor of Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Response in Obstructive Sleep Apnoea-A Systematic Review · 0:22:43
    A systematic review of eight Inspire cohorts, 1,051 patients in all, asked whether complete collapse of the lateral pharyngeal walls on sleep endoscopy predicts a poor response to hypoglossal nerve stimulation. It lowers the response rate by roughly 15 to 20 percentage points, yet about two of three such patients still respond, so it is a counseling point rather than an exclusion. Call: watch.

    McBrinn S, Skan O, Iacovidou A. "Oropharyngeal Lateral Wall Collapse on Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy as a Predictor of Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Response in Obstructive Sleep Apnoea—A Systematic Review." *Diagnostics* 2026;16:2529 (20 pages; issue number not printed).   • 2026-10-06 — Edema and Subjective Discomfort After Barbed Stayed Bridge Pharyngoplasty (BSBP) · 0:15:10
    A prospective Italian cohort scored palatal swelling and discomfort day by day after barbed stayed bridge pharyngoplasty, a variant of barbed palate surgery. Swelling and pain peaked in the first day and improved markedly by 48 hours, which sharpens recovery counseling, but the technique was not compared with standard barbed reposition pharyngoplasty. Call: watch; no change in technique.

    Pace A, Iannella G, Maniaci A, Cocuzza S, Moffa A, Alunni Fegatelli D, et al. Edema and Subjective Discomfort After Barbed Stayed Bridge Pharyngoplasty (BSBP). *JCM*. 2025;14(20):7402. doi:10.3390/jcm14207402
  • 2026-10-05 — Consensus Statements Among European Sleep Surgery Experts on Tongue, Hypopharynx, and Supraglottis Associated With Snoring and Obstructive Sleep Apnea - Part 1 - Evaluation and Decision Making · 0:16:51
    A European expert panel reached consensus on how to evaluate the tongue base, hypopharynx and supraglottis in snoring and sleep apnea, and how those findings should steer treatment. The statements support staging palate surgery before hypoglossal nerve stimulation when large tonsils coexist with tongue base collapse, and repeating sleep endoscopy before any revision surgery. Call: watch; the workup stays as it is.

    Olszewska E, De Vito A, Baptista P, Delakorda M, Heiser C, Cheong RCT, et al. Consensus Statements Among European Sleep Surgery Experts on Tongue, Hypopharynx, and Supraglottis Associated with Snoring and Obstructive Sleep Apnea: Part 1: Evaluation and Decision Making. *JCM*. 2025;15(1):80. doi:10.3390/jcm15010080
