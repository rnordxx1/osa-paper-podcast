Every day, one new paper on obstructive sleep apnea surgery: hypoglossal nerve stimulation, palate surgery including barbed reposition pharyngoplasty, tongue base, skeletal and nasal procedures, patient selection and comparators. Every episode summarizes the paper, walks through it as a teaching round, places it against a 440-paper evidence library, and ends with whether it should change practice. Episodes are written and narrated by an AI system working from the full text of each paper and the host's curated library of digests, under rules set by the host. They are reviewed for teaching, not for patient care. Nothing here is medical advice. Episode guide and subscribe links: https://www.sleepapneaimplant.org/podcast Produced by Ryan Nord, MD (VCU Health). Disclosures: consultant to AvivoMed; previously a consultant to Inspire Medical Systems (ended 2025) and Medtronic. No company funds or reviews this podcast.

  • 2026-11-11 — Comparing Transoral Robotic Surgery and Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Using Mean Disease Alleviation in OSA · 0:12:54
    A single-center retrospective study from Thomas Jefferson University compared robotic lingual tonsillectomy with Inspire hypoglossal nerve stimulation in patients who could not tolerate continuous positive airway pressure, using mean disease alleviation, a measure that combines apnea-hypopnea index improvement with nightly use. After matching, the two operations showed no statistically significant difference, but the robotic surgery group was small (32 patients), younger and selected for different airway anatomy. It supports choosing between the operations by airway anatomy, but it cannot show that they are equivalent.

    Mandloi S, DeKloe J, Kaki P, Shah R, Estephan LE, Naimi B, et al. Comparing Transoral Robotic Surgery and Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Using Mean Disease Alleviation in OSA. *Ann Otol Rhinol Laryngol*. 2026;135(4):262-267. doi:10.1177/00034894251388836
  • 2026-11-10 — CPAP Treatment Persistence Following Nasal Procedures in Obstructive Sleep Apnea - A Real-World Claims-Based Analysis of 1.3 Million Patients · 0:14:03
    A study drawing on a very large United States insurance claims database asked whether nasal procedures performed after starting continuous positive airway pressure were linked to patients staying on the therapy. Patients who had an office-based temperature-controlled radiofrequency treatment of the nasal valve stopped therapy at a lower rate than those without nasal surgery (hazard ratio 0.62), with smaller associations for septoplasty and for turbinate or sinus surgery, and no statistically significant association for functional rhinoplasty alone. The finding is one to watch: it supports examining the nose before declaring positive airway pressure a failure, but the design cannot show that the procedure caused the difference.

    Malhotra A, Takashima M, Vahabzadeh-Hagh A, Yaremchuk K, Niklewski P. CPAP treatment persistence following nasal procedures in obstructive sleep apnea: a real-world claims-based analysis of 1.3 million patients. *Journal of Medical Economics*. 2026;29(1):1474–1486. doi:10.1080/13696998.2026.2671550
  • 2026-11-09 — SNORES Classification - Multicenter Prospective Validation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:47
    A three-center prospective cohort study from Pakistan asked whether SNORES, a six-domain score built from sleep study, nasal airflow, breathing effort, awake endoscopy and sleep position data, predicts later treatment escalation, surgery and adherence to continuous positive airway pressure better than the apnea-hypopnea index alone. The score ranked patients for later therapy escalation far better than the apnea-hypopnea index (area under the curve 0.82), though the paper is inconsistent about whether that figure comes from all 1,560 patients or a 390-patient subset; those outcomes were also clinicians' decisions partly based on the same findings, and the study reported no procedure-specific surgical results. It is one to watch: useful as a structured clinic descriptor, but not yet a reason to alter surgical candidacy workups or to replace drug-induced sleep endoscopy.

    Eishal Arshad, Junaid Iqbal, Aisha Shafique. SNORES Classification: Multicenter Prospective Validation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *The Laryngoscope*. 2026;136(10):4564–4574 (issue number from the Wiley download banner). doi:10.1002/lary.70549
  • 2026-11-08 — Challenging Barriers in OSA - Outcomes of Down Under Pharyngoplasty and Coblation Tongue Channelling · 0:13:25
    A two-center Australian cohort from Peninsula Health asked whether adults with obstructive sleep apnea and so-called unfavorable throat anatomy can still benefit from a modified barbed reposition pharyngoplasty combined with coblation tongue channelling. Among patients who had a follow-up sleep study, about three in four met standard criteria for surgical success, including many with anatomy traditionally thought to predict failure. Because the study had no control group, short follow-up and incomplete sleep-study data, the finding is a promising signal to watch rather than proof.

    Nasserallah MV, Monna M, Edwards Z, Lum M, Tobin V, Mahanta VR, Diamantaras D, Shetty S, Mulder J, de Silva N. Challenging barriers in OSA: Outcomes of 'Down under' pharyngoplasty and coblation tongue channelling. *European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology*. 2026;283:3979–3990. doi:10.1007/s00405-026-10149-4
  • 2026-11-07 — Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation and the Incidence of Cardiovascular Disease · 0:13:09
    A University of Chicago study in JAMA Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery used a national commercial insurance database to ask whether hypoglossal nerve stimulation is linked to fewer new cardiovascular diagnoses in patients with obstructive sleep apnea who could not use positive airway pressure. With longer follow-up, implanted patients had about half the hazard of a new hypertension diagnosis, but early on they had more new hypertension diagnoses, and heart attack, stroke and heart failure were not significantly reduced. The finding is one to watch: it informs patient counseling but does not support offering the implant for cardiovascular protection.

    Kondamuri N, Hyman MJ, Cai Y, LoSavio PS, Skolarus TA. Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation and the Incidence of Cardiovascular Disease. *JAMA Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg*. 2026;. doi:10.1001/jamaoto.2026.1777
  • 2026-11-06 — Low Arousal Threshold and Long-Term Adherence to Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation · 0:13:19
    A two-center retrospective study of Inspire hypoglossal nerve stimulation patients asked whether a low respiratory arousal threshold, estimated from routine sleep study reports, predicts falling away from therapy. Early adherence was similar, but by six months low-threshold patients were far more likely to have lapsed, mostly by skipping whole nights. The take-home point: the signal supports closer counseling and follow-up for these patients, not a different decision about who is offered an implant.

    Kaki PC, Ravuri S, Patel SD, Kim J, Edwards BA, Vena D, et al. Low Arousal Threshold and Long-Term Adherence to Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation. *JAMA Otolaryngology–Head & Neck Surgery*. 2026; published online September 24, 2026; pages E1–E9. doi:10.1001/jamaoto.2026.2816
  • 2026-11-05 — Adenotonsillectomy for Children With Mild Sleep-Disordered Breathing - Who Benefits? · 0:13:45
    A secondary analysis of the Pediatric Adenotonsillectomy Trial for Snoring asked whether children who snore but show little or no obstructive sleep apnea on a sleep study benefit from early adenotonsillectomy, and whether any feature known before surgery predicts who benefits. Surgery beat watchful waiting on a combined measure of behavior, quality of life, sleepiness and blood pressure at one year, with one extra child meaningfully improved for every four treated, but almost no baseline feature predicted benefit apart from a possible signal from a high Mallampati score. The study supports counseling families that a near-normal sleep study need not by itself rule out surgery, while the Mallampati signal is one to watch until it is replicated.

    Chervin RD, Gueye-Ndiaye S, Cen M, Mitchell RB, Liu C, Ibrahim S, et al. Adenotonsillectomy for children with mild sleep-disordered breathing: who benefits? *SLEEP*. 2026;49(10):zsag169 (pp. doi:10.1093/sleep/zsag169
  • 2026-11-04 — Phrenic Nerve Stimulation Enhances Upper Airway Patency During Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy in Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:28
    A 2026 study in the Annals of the American Thoracic Society asked whether stimulating the phrenic nerve through the skin, timed to different points in the breath, could improve upper airway flow in sedated patients with obstructive sleep apnea undergoing drug-induced sleep endoscopy. In 13 patients, a single burst at the end of expiration raised peak inspiratory flow by about 64 percent compared with unstimulated breaths, while bursts late in the breath did not help. Because the study measured no apnea-hypopnea index and no natural sleep, it is a mechanism finding to watch rather than a reason to change who is offered surgery.

    Yu JL, Gouldman KP, Kane BL, Manning KN, McEachran MC, Bourn D, Schneider H, Schwartz AR. Phrenic nerve stimulation enhances upper airway patency during drug-induced sleep endoscopy in obstructive sleep apnea. *Annals of the American Thoracic Society*. 2026;23(8):1207–1216. doi:10.1093/annalsats/aaoag079
  • 2026-11-03 — Effect of Novel Hypoglossal Stimulation on Airflow and Airway Collapsibility in OSA · 0:13:48
    A study in CHEST from Eckert's group at Flinders University asked whether a temporary electrode, placed through the skin under ultrasound guidance next to the hypoglossal nerve, could reopen the airway in sedated adults with obstructive sleep apnea during drug-induced sleep endoscopy. Stimulation restored airflow almost to the level reached on continuous positive airway pressure in thirteen of fourteen participants. With no natural sleep, apnea-hypopnea index or implantable device studied, the approach is one to watch rather than a reason to change who is offered hypoglossal nerve stimulation today.

    Osman AM, Carney AS, Ooi EH, Joshi H, Toson B, Tran C, Hudson AL, Nguyen P, Gelekis N, Eastwood PR, Catcheside P, Eckert DJ. Effect of Novel Hypoglossal Stimulation on Airflow and Airway Collapsibility in OSA. *CHEST*. 2026;169(1):257-268. doi:10.1016/j.chest.2025.08.020
  • 2026-11-02 — Physiologic Mechanisms of Response to Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation · 0:13:25
    Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania asked whether airway physiology measured during drug-induced sleep endoscopy explains why some patients respond to hypoglossal nerve stimulation and others do not. In thirty patients with Inspire implants, the drop in airway opening pressure when the stimulator was switched on tracked the fall in the back-sleeping apnea-hypopnea index, with a strong correlation of 0.70, while measurements taken before stimulation did not separate responders from nonresponders. The verdict is watch: the finding helps explain response and frames the endoscopy workup of non-responders, but it does not yet provide a test for choosing patients before implant.

    Magaña LC, Dixit Y, Saconi B, Keenan BT, Seay EG, Schwartz AR, et al. Physiologic mechanisms of response to hypoglossal nerve stimulation. *SLEEP*. 2026;00:1–9, advance access 25 August 2026 (volume/issue not yet assigned). doi:10.1093/sleep/zsag227
  • 2026-11-01 — Association Between Body Mass Index and Unilateral Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Efficacy Depends on the Pattern of Pharyngeal Collapse in Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:50
    A 2026 study in the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine asked whether body mass index affects the results of hypoglossal nerve stimulation differently depending on the pattern of airway collapse seen on drug-induced sleep endoscopy. In patients whose airway walls collapsed from the sides, higher body mass index was linked to substantially less reduction in the apnea-hypopnea index, while in front-to-back collapse the effect was small and not statistically significant, though the side-wall group held only sixty patients. What it adds: the finding supports counseling by collapse pattern rather than body mass index alone, but it needs independent confirmation.

    Vena D, Kezirian EJ, Wellman A, Kent D, D’Agostino M, Monteiro JLGC, et al. Association between body mass index and unilateral hypoglossal nerve stimulation efficacy depends on the pattern of pharyngeal collapse in obstructive sleep apnea. *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*. 2026;. doi:10.1093/ajrccm/aamag427
  • 2026-10-30 — Anthropometric Measurements Inform Complete Concentric Collapse Status in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:34
    A prospective multicenter study from Weiner's group in OTO Open asked whether body mass index, neck circumference and supine pharyngeal width could predict complete concentric collapse on drug-induced sleep endoscopy in candidates for hypoglossal nerve stimulation. A decision tree combining body mass index and neck circumference was right about 96 percent of the time when it called a patient clear, only modestly better than assuming no one had the finding. The finding is one to watch: sleep endoscopy remains part of candidacy screening, while these simple measurements offer useful numbers for counseling.

    Weiner JS, Chio EG, Dedhia RC, Huntley CT, Hutz MJ, Huyett P, et al. Anthropometric Measurements Inform Complete Concentric Collapse Status in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *OTO Open*. 2026;10(2):e70245. doi:10.1002/oto2.70245
  • 2026-10-29 — Deep Learning-Based Automatic Scoring of Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy in Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:42
    A five-hospital Korean study asked whether a deep learning model could grade drug-induced sleep endoscopy videos the way an expert panel does, scoring both the degree of airway obstruction and its primary cause. On videos from hospitals not used in training, the model matched expert grading of soft palate obstruction in 82.4 percent of 717 clips, with weaker performance lower in the airway, especially at the epiglottis. It is a tool to watch: it cannot grade concentric collapse and has not been linked to treatment outcomes, so it does not yet inform hypoglossal nerve stimulation candidacy.

    Kim JY, Mun SJ, Baik YS, Lee YS, Kim YJ, Oh J, et al. Deep learning-based automatic scoring of drug-induced sleep endoscopy in obstructive sleep apnea. *npj Digital Medicine*. 2026; article number, no issue). doi:10.1038/s41746-026-02673-8
  • 2026-10-28 — Subjective and objective measures of sleep-related function from the Cardiovascular Endpoints For Obstructive Sleep Apnea with Twelfth Cranial Nerve Stimulation (CARDIOSA-12) trial: Sleep-related functional outcomes in CARDIOSA-12 · 0:13:43
    A secondary analysis of the Cardiovascular Endpoints for Obstructive Sleep Apnea with Twelfth Cranial Nerve Stimulation (CARDIOSA-12) randomized crossover trial asked whether full versus partial hypoglossal nerve stimulation improves objective cognition and patient-reported sleep outcomes in obstructive sleep apnea. Sleepiness, insomnia, snoring and daily-function questionnaire scores improved on full stimulation, with the Epworth sleepiness score about 1.75 points lower, but reaction-time and processing-speed tests showed no statistically significant difference in the main analysis. The findings leave candidacy as it was but support counseling patients to expect feeling better, not measurably sharper thinking.

    Tangutur A, Yu PK, Basner M, Keenan BT, Dedhia RC. Subjective and objective measures of sleep-related function from the Cardiovascular Endpoints For Obstructive Sleep Apnea with Twelfth Cranial Nerve Stimulation (CARDIOSA-12) trial: Sleep-related functional outcomes in CARDIOSA-12. *J Clin Sleep Med*. 2026;22(1). doi:10.1007/s44470-026-00067-x
  • 2026-10-27 — Three-Year Outcomes of Proximal Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation in OSA · 0:13:18
    Schwartz and colleagues, with Maurer as senior author, asked whether proximal hypoglossal nerve stimulation, a LivaNova device distinct from Inspire, keeps working at three years in the Targeted Hypoglossal Neurostimulation 3 trial. In the patients who completed a three-year sleep study, the median apnea-hypopnea index was roughly halved and held steady from year one, though about one in four patients did not finish follow-up and no three-year responder rate was reported. Take-home point: useful counseling evidence for patients asking about the other nerve stimulator, but not a reason to change Inspire candidacy criteria.

    Schwartz AR, Jacobowitz O, Mickelson SA, Miller MB, Oliven A, Certal V, et al. Three-Year Outcomes of Proximal Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation in OSA. *Chest*. 2026;169(5):1357-1370. doi:10.1016/j.chest.2025.11.057
  • 2026-10-27 — Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation and Hypoxic Burden in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Secondary Analysis of the STAR Trial · 0:13:42
    A secondary analysis of the Stimulation Therapy for Apnea Reduction trial asked whether hypoglossal nerve stimulation with a first-generation Inspire device lowers hypoxic burden, a measure of how deep and how long nighttime oxygen drops are. About seven in ten patients had their hypoxic burden cut by more than half at one year, though the study had no control group and measured no heart events. The finding is one to watch and adds a useful counseling point, but it does not change who is offered implantation.

    Xu J, Hajipour Z, Aloia MS, de Beeck SO, Vanderveken OM, Huyett P, et al. Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation and Hypoxic Burden in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Secondary Analysis of the STAR Trial. *JAMA Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg*. 2026;152(7):696-704. doi:10.1001/jamaoto.2026.1049
  • 2026-10-24 — Proximal Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea in the OSPREY Study: A Randomized Controlled Trial · 0:13:43
    The OSPREY randomized trial tested whether an investigational proximal hypoglossal nerve stimulator, the LivaNova aura6000, works better than the same device implanted but switched off in adults with moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea who could not tolerate positive airway pressure. At seven months, 58.2 percent of 67 treated patients met the response criteria, compared with 13.5 percent of 37 controls, with no serious device-related adverse events. The trial gives surgeons a randomized benchmark for counseling patients about an investigational alternative, but it is not Inspire evidence and does not address complete concentric collapse, so it is one to watch.

    Malhotra A, Schwartz AR, Lovett E, Juran N, Nguyen SA, Barrera JE, et al. Proximal Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea in the OSPREY Study: A Randomized Controlled Trial. *Ann Intern Med*. 2026;179(6):812-822. doi:10.7326/ANNALS-25-04414
  • 2026-10-21 — A Randomized Trial of Nefopam for Postoperative Pain Management in Obstructive Sleep Apnea Patients Undergoing Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty · 0:14:18
    Chumpathong and colleagues at Siriraj Hospital in Bangkok ran a small randomized, placebo-controlled trial asking whether nefopam, a non-opioid pain drug not approved in the United States, reduces morphine use in the first day after uvulopalatopharyngoplasty for obstructive sleep apnea. Added to a strong multimodal pain regimen, it did not: both groups needed only a few milligrams of morphine, with no statistically significant difference. Because the trial was underpowered, it supports not adding nefopam routinely while leaving a modest effect unexcluded.

    Chumpathong S, Mangmeesri P, Panyamee S, Banhiran W, Uataya M. A randomized trial of nefopam for postoperative pain management in obstructive sleep apnea patients undergoing uvulopalatopharyngoplasty. *European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology*. 2026. doi:10.1007/s00405-026-10472-w
  • 2026-10-21 — Systematic Review on the Safety of Outpatient Upper Airway Surgery for Obstructive Sleep Apnea Patients in Ambulatory Surgical Centers · 0:12:50
    A 2025 systematic review from Rotenberg's group asked whether adults with obstructive sleep apnea can safely have nasal and palate surgery in ambulatory surgical centers and go home the same day. Across mostly older, hospital-based studies, unplanned admission for medical reasons averaged 5.4 percent across the four studies that reported it, above the authors' own 3 percent benchmark, and serious events were rare and came within the first few hours. The take-home point: the review maps early risk usefully but contains almost no true surgery-center data, so it does not yet justify moving pharyngoplasty patients out of overnight observation.

    Asghar M, Pang K, Asghar M, Rotenberg B. Systematic Review on the Safety of Outpatient Upper Airway Surgery for Obstructive Sleep Apnea Patients in Ambulatory Surgical Centers. *Journal of Otolaryngology - Head & Neck Surgery*. 2025;54. doi:10.1177/19160216251379325
  • 2026-10-20 — Association Between Perioperative Celecoxib, Acetaminophen, and Opioid Consumption After Palate Surgery for Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:14:02
    A single-surgeon retrospective study by Badash and colleagues, with Kezirian as senior author, asked whether celecoxib or acetaminophen given around palate surgery for obstructive sleep apnea was associated with less opioid use in the first day after surgery. Higher celecoxib doses were associated with modestly lower opioid use, while acetaminophen showed no significant association, and 205 of 210 patients still received opioids. Because celecoxib became routine partway through the study period, the finding is one to watch for confirmation in a randomized trial.

    Badash I, Wong S, Biju K, Hur K, Commesso E, Kezirian EJ. Association Between Perioperative Celecoxib, Acetaminophen, and Opioid Consumption After Palate Surgery for Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery*. 2025;172(2):678–685. doi:10.1002/ohn.1069
  • 2026-10-18 — Predictive Factors for Surgical Outcomes and Symptom Persistence After Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:19
    A single-center study from Wujin Hospital in China asked which preoperative features predict success after a modified uvulopalatopharyngoplasty for obstructive sleep apnea. About two in three patients met the success definition at short-term follow-up, and a lower Friedman stage, lower body mass index and higher lowest oxygen saturation were linked to success. The proposed prediction model has not been validated outside the patients who built it, so it is one to watch.

    Ling X, Zheng J, Liu Q, Shi C, Fan C. Predictive factors for surgical outcomes and symptom persistence after uvulopalatopharyngoplasty in patients with obstructive sleep apnea. *Head Face Med*. 2026;22(1). doi:10.1186/s13005-026-00621-1
  • 2026-10-17 — Uvularis Muscle Strengthening Advancement in Palatopharyngeal Collapse in Selected Cases of Snoring and Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Randomized Trial · 0:13:57
    A randomized trial from Zagazig University in Egypt asked whether adding a uvularis muscle advancement stitch to modified anterior palatoplasty improves outcomes in young adults with mild or early moderate obstructive sleep apnea and single-level, front-to-back palatal collapse. In 34 patients, the added stitch did not significantly lower the apnea-hypopnea index compared with palatoplasty alone, although bed-partner-rated snoring improved more. The snoring benefit is one to watch, pending a blinded, registered trial.

    Askar SM, Awad AM, Abou-Sharkh AA. Uvularis muscle strengthening advancement in palatopharyngeal collapse in selected cases of snoring and obstructive sleep apnea: a randomized trial. *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol*. 2026;283(1):527-534. doi:10.1007/s00405-025-09844-5
  • 2026-10-16 — Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty Versus Expansion Sphincter Pharyngoplasty - A Single Centre Experience · 0:13:50
    A retrospective single-center study from the Medical University of Innsbruck compared expansion sphincter pharyngoplasty with a muscle-sparing uvulopalatopharyngoplasty in patients with snoring or obstructive sleep apnea, with the operation chosen by drug-induced sedation endoscopy findings. Expansion sphincter pharyngoplasty significantly lowered the apnea-hypopnea index, to about 13 events per hour, while the uvulopalatopharyngoplasty group's fall was not significant; expansion sphincter pharyngoplasty also brought more minor bleeding and more strong pain medication. The study adds independent support for palate operations that reshape the lateral pharyngeal wall and gives frank morbidity numbers for counseling, but it cannot prove superiority and does not test barbed reposition pharyngoplasty.

    Steinbichler TB, Bender B, Hartl R, Strasser V, Sontheimer D, Buricic S, et al. Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty Versus Expansion Sphincter Pharyngoplasty: A Single Centre Experience. *Clocks & Sleep*. 2025;7(3):38. doi:10.3390/clockssleep7030038
  • 2026-10-15 — Auditory Perceptions Associated With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea · 0:13:34
    Researchers at Virginia Commonwealth University surveyed Inspire hypoglossal nerve stimulation recipients by telephone to ask whether they hear new sounds when the device stimulates. About one in six reported a new buzzing or humming sound during stimulation, mostly mild, and none had stopped using the device; preexisting tinnitus was not a statistically significant predictor in this small sample. The finding changes how patients are counseled and questioned about sound at follow-up, not who is offered the implant.

    Belilos A, Adibi I, Tucker A, Saeedi A, Calder A, Lee L, Nord R. Auditory Perceptions Associated With Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology*. 2026;11:e70550. doi:10.1002/lio2.70550
  • 2026-10-15 — Preservation Palatopharyngoplasty for Obstructive Sleep Apnea: Predictors and Clinical Outcomes · 0:14:14
    A single-surgeon retrospective series from the Medical University of South Carolina asked how adults with moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea fare after preservation palatopharyngoplasty, and which baseline features predict a larger drop in the apnea-hypopnea index. The apnea-hypopnea index was cut roughly in half, with larger tonsils and higher baseline severity predicting bigger drops, though only a minority of patients were cured. The take-home point: the paper sharpens counseling for patients with large tonsils who fall outside Inspire candidacy, but it needs controlled, longer-term confirmation.

    Abdelwahab M, Alhussien A, Kassir MF, Butehorn H, Munhall C, Nguyen SA. Preservation Palatopharyngoplasty for Obstructive Sleep Apnea: Predictors and Clinical Outcomes. *Otolaryngol.--head neck surg.*. 2026;174(4):1102-1108. doi:10.1002/ohn.70183
  • 2026-10-14 — Post-Operative Radiological Evaluation of a Bilateral Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation System · 0:14:00
    A single-center German case series of eleven patients asked what the first postoperative image should be after implanting the Genio bilateral hypoglossal nerve stimulator. Plain x-rays showed the hardware cleanly in every patient, while every computed tomography scan carried metal artifact, and the paper reported no clinical outcomes linking paddle position to function. The take-home point: plain films make a sensible day-one baseline, and repeat imaging over time is needed to show whether the paddles migrate.

    Kilgue A, Gehl H-B, Abolmaali N, Scholtz L-U, Scherl C, Todt I. Post-operative radiological evaluation of a bilateral hypoglossal nerve stimulation system. *Front. Surg.* 2026;13:1818247. doi:10.3389/fsurg.2026.1818247
  • 2026-10-14 — Exploring the Role of Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy in Optimizing Upper Airway Stimulation Therapy for Non-Responders · 0:13:45
    Copper's group at Sint Antonius Hospital in the Netherlands described how they use drug-induced sleep endoscopy with the stimulator switched on in patients who still fail Inspire hypoglossal nerve stimulation after standard titration, and what happened to those patients afterwards. In a small retrospective series without a control group, patients who had settings changes or added therapy and returned for follow-up saw their apnea-hypopnea index fall by about 18 events per hour. Dr. Nord considers the procedure a good tool for the tolerant non-responder once awake endoscopy and reprogramming have failed, while noting that the series cannot show the endoscopy itself caused the improvement.

    Kant E, Hardeman JA, Stokroos RJ, Copper MP. Exploring the role of drug-induced sleep endoscopy in optimizing upper airway stimulation therapy for non-responders. *Sleep Breath*. 2025;29(5). doi:10.1007/s11325-025-03468-z
  • 2026-10-14 — GLP-1 receptor agonists for obstructive sleep apnea: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials · 0:14:28
    A meta-analysis in Sleep and Breathing pooled randomized trials of liraglutide and tirzepatide in adults with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea. The drugs lowered the apnea-hypopnea index by about ten events per hour more than control, but the effect depended heavily on the drug, and pooled oxygen measures did not improve significantly. The paper does not change who is offered surgery, but it sharpens counseling: quote drug-specific numbers, re-test after weight loss, and, as in Dr. Nord's practice, treat weight-loss drugs and Inspire as complementary parts of treatment, though no trial has yet tested them together.

    Carvalhal G, Mora MMR, Peralta-Jiménez GA, Ayasa L, Barrera V, Advani K, et al. GLP-1 receptor agonists for obstructive sleep apnea: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. *Sleep Breath*. 2026. doi:10.1007/s11325-026-03826-5
  • 2026-10-14 — Edema and Subjective Discomfort After Barbed Stayed Bridge Pharyngoplasty (BSBP) · 0:13:31
    Researchers at Sapienza University of Rome followed a small, carefully selected group of patients with moderate obstructive sleep apnea after barbed stayed bridge pharyngoplasty, a muscle-sparing barbed palate operation, to measure early pain, swelling and discomfort. Pain stayed low, averaging about three out of ten on the first day and near zero by one month, with no bleeding, but without a comparison group the study cannot show the technique outperforms barbed reposition pharyngoplasty. Dr. Nord's take-home point: a promising early-recovery signal for one technique in thirty selected patients, still awaiting a head-to-head comparison with barbed reposition pharyngoplasty.

    Pace A, Iannella G, Maniaci A, Cocuzza S, Moffa A, Alunni Fegatelli D, et al. Edema and Subjective Discomfort After Barbed Stayed Bridge Pharyngoplasty (BSBP). *JCM*. 2025;14(20):7402. doi:10.3390/jcm14207402
  • 2026-10-14 — Hypoxic Burden Reduction After Lateral Pharyngoplasty in Patients With Obstructive Sleep Apnea - Beyond the Apnea-Hypopnea Index · 0:13:54
    A single-center study from the Pontifical Catholic University of Campinas in Brazil asked whether lateral pharyngoplasty lowers hypoxic burden, a measure of nighttime oxygen loss, and not just the apnea-hypopnea index. In the 27 patients with hypoxic burden measured before and after surgery, median hypoxic burden fell to about a third of baseline.
